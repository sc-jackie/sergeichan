How it works

Accounts feed one book you can check from your phone.

  1. 01

    Connect sources

    Connect the accounts you already use: exchanges, wallets, IBKR, Hyperliquid, cards, cash pots, property values, receivables, and other people’s money (OPM). Connections are read-only where possible and manual where needed. Newfin never gets a signing key.

    • On-chain wallets alongside cards and cash
    • Brokerages and trading venues (IBKR, Hyperliquid)
    • Property, receivables, and other people’s money in the same book
  2. 02

    Reconcile into one book

    Newfin brings those sources into one household ledger. It matches transfers against spending, so moving money from a wallet to an exchange doesn’t look like income, and withdrawing from an exchange to a card doesn’t look like a purchase. Scheduled syncs keep the book current.

    • Transfers detected and excluded from spending
    • Cards + on-chain cash treated as one cash surface
    • Scheduled background syncs keep balances current
  3. 03

    See liquid beside locked

    Every position is marked liquid, locked, vesting, or illiquid. Net worth and spendable money stay as separate numbers on the same screen.

    • Vesting kept out of “spendable”
    • Property valued but flagged illiquid
    • Receivables and OPM separated from own capital
  4. 04

    Check cashflow and trading in context

    Cashflow shows the household’s actual spending and income across every card and wallet. The live desk places open IBKR and Hyperliquid positions beside that liquid cushion, so you can size a position against money the household can use.

    • Household cashflow across cards + on-chain
    • Open positions from IBKR and Hyperliquid
    • Position size compared with the household’s liquid cushion
  5. 05

    Ask Persik from Telegram

    Persik gives you access to the same book through Telegram. Ask what’s liquid, what came in this month, or what a position is worth from your phone. Persik sees only what your role allows and cannot move money.

    • The same backend as the web app, not a separate copy
    • Role scoping applied before the model sees anything
    • Read-only: no keys, no trades, no transfers

One backend, two doors.

The web app and Persik share the same Postgres database and role rules, so both stay on one household ledger.

Read-only AI
Persik and any model can read permitted household data without signing keys. They cannot move money.
Role before model
Access rules apply when the payload is assembled, before an answer is generated. Permissions are enforced as a boundary, not a prompt instruction.
Portable by default
Your household book is exportable. It’s a private ledger available through these surfaces, not a walled garden.
